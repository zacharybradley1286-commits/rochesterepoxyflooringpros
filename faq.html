Epoxy Flooring FAQ

Straight answers on cost, cure times, slab condition and maintenance for epoxy and polyaspartic floors in Rochester and South Thurston County.

Cost & quoting

How much does an epoxy garage floor cost in the Rochester area?

A complete professional system generally runs $5.50 to $8.50 per square foot. A typical two-car garage of 400–500 sq ft lands between $2,200 and $3,800, depending on slab condition and the system specified.

Why is a professional quote so much higher than a big-box kit?

Different products and different preparation. A retail kit is a 30–40% solids water-based coating applied over an acid etch. A professional system is 100% solids epoxy over a diamond-ground profile, with a polyaspartic topcoat. The kit typically lasts one to two years; the professional system lasts 15 to 25 or more.

Is the estimate free, and is it binding?

Estimates are free. Pricing is confirmed after the slab is measured and inspected, because slab condition is the largest single variable. Ask for the scope in writing with the grinding method, moisture test result, resin system and mil thickness itemized.

Durability & performance

How long will the floor last?

A properly ground and installed 100% solids epoxy with a polyaspartic topcoat typically lasts 15 to 25+ years in a residential garage with routine cleaning.

Will hot tires pull the coating up?

Not on a thermoset system. Hot-tire pickup happens with thin, low-solids coatings that soften under heat. 100% solids epoxy cures to a rigid thermoset that does not re-soften.

Will the floor turn yellow?

Epoxy ambers with UV exposure over time, which is why a UV-stable aliphatic polyaspartic topcoat is specified over it. In a garage with significant direct sunlight through an open door, that topcoat is what preserves the color.

What if my slab cracks later?

A coating is a surface system, not structural. If the slab moves, the coating will reflect it. Existing cracks are routed and filled with flexible polyurea before coating, which handles normal hairline cracking; active structural movement is a slab issue first.

Scheduling & the install itself

How long does installation take?

Most residential garages take one to two days: grind and repair, then base coat and topcoat.

When can I walk on it and park on it?

Light foot traffic is typically fine 12 to 24 hours after the topcoat. Vehicles want roughly 72 hours on a standard epoxy system, and less with a polyaspartic topcoat. Cold weather extends both.

Do I need to empty the garage?

Yes. The floor needs to be completely clear — vehicles, shelving, stored items and anything mounted low on the walls. Plan for the space to be unusable for the duration plus the cure window.

Can this be done in winter?

Often, but slab temperature governs it, not the calendar. Standard epoxy slows sharply below about 60°F. Cold-month projects use temporary heat and containment, or a polyaspartic formulated for low-temperature cure.

Is the process dusty?

Grinders run with HEPA vacuum extraction capturing dust at the source, so very little escapes. This matters for health as well as cleanup — concrete dust contains crystalline silica, a regulated respiratory hazard.

Does it smell?

100% solids epoxy is very low odor compared to solvent-based products. Polyaspartic topcoats have a stronger odor during application but cure quickly. Ventilation is used throughout, and attached garages are typically fine within a day.

Slab condition & suitability

Can you coat a new concrete slab?

New concrete needs to cure before coating — generally about 28 days — so residual moisture from the pour can leave. Coating too early traps that moisture under the film and causes blistering.

My floor already has a coating that is peeling. Can you go over it?

No. A failing coating is removed mechanically during grinding. A new coating bonds only as well as whatever it sits on, so coating over a lifting film just relocates the failure.

There are oil stains. Is that a problem?

Surface staining is not, but oil that has soaked deep into the slab will reject a coating. Contaminated areas are degreased and, where needed, ground deeper before primer.

Why does moisture testing matter so much here?

Much of this region sits on a high seasonal water table, and older slabs frequently lack a working vapor barrier underneath. Moisture vapor pushes upward continuously and collects under an impermeable coating, releasing it as blisters or peeling. Testing before primer determines whether a vapor-barrier base coat is required.

Maintenance

How do I clean it?

Dust mop or blow out grit, then damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid citrus and vinegar-based cleaners, which dull the topcoat over time. Use a plastic blade rather than steel for scraping.

Can it be recoated later?

Yes. A sound epoxy floor can be lightly abraded and given a fresh topcoat to restore the wear surface and sheen, which is far less involved and less expensive than a full replacement.
