Repairing Spalled & Salt-Damaged Winter Concrete Slabs in Rochester

By Rochester Epoxy Flooring Pros Editorial Team

Restore pitted, crumbling, salt-damaged concrete slabs with high-strength polymer resurfacing mortars before installing decorative epoxy coatings.

Quick Answer · Direct Summary

Concrete spalling caused by freeze-thaw cycles and road deicing salts can be permanently restored using high-strength polymer-modified cementitious mortars and deep-penetrating epoxy primers.

Next Question (What causes garage floor pitting in Western Washington?): Vehicles track road salt and deicers into the garage. Salt lowers water's freezing point, causing rapid freeze-thaw cycles that pop brittle concrete surface aggregate out of the slab.

Pitted, scaling, and crumbling concrete garage floors are an all-too-common sight across Rochester, Grand Mound, and Thurston County. Slabs that were once smooth and level develop rough, sandpaper-like craters, flaking surface crusts, and deep pockmarks that constantly shed fine gray concrete dust.

This deterioration is known as concrete spalling. It occurs when winter road deicing salts and brine tracked in from Highway 12, I-5, and rural country roads melt onto your garage floor. The saline water penetrates concrete pores, crystallizes, and undergoes intense hydraulic pressure when temperatures dip below freezing. This pressure forces the top 1/8 to 1/4 inch of concrete paste to fracture and pop off.

Many homeowners try to fix pitted concrete by pouring retail self-leveling underlayments or spreading thin sand-mix patches over the holes. Without proper mechanical diamond grinding and bonding agents, these DIY patches quickly crack, loosen, and turn to gravel under vehicle tires.

Our restoration process completely resurrects heavily spalled concrete. We begin with aggressive diamond grinding using specialized coarse-grit metal-bond tooling to grind away all soft, decayed concrete until solid, competent aggregate is exposed across the entire slab.

We then apply commercial-grade fast-setting polyurea and epoxy resurfacing mortars to rebuild craters flush with the original elevation. Once cured, the entire surface is ground flat and encapsulated in our 100% solids moisture barrier and full-broadcast flake system, creating a brand-new floor that will never scale or pit again.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my concrete floor too pitted and damaged to be saved with epoxy?

Almost never. Unless a slab has suffered total structural collapse or large subterranean voids, heavy diamond grinding and polymer repair mortars can restore even severely pitted floors.

Will road deicing salts damage my new polyaspartic coating next winter?

No. Commercial polyaspartic topcoats are completely non-porous and chemically impervious to magnesium chloride, road salt, and winter deicers, preventing future spalling.
