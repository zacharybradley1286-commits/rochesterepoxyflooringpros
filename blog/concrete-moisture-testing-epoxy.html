Concrete Moisture Testing: Preventing Floor Coating Blisters in the Chehalis Basin

By Epoxy Flooring in Rochester Washington Editorial Team

Learn how high water tables and hydrostatic pressure in Rochester trigger coating blisters, and how moisture mitigation primers stop failure.

Quick Answer · Direct Summary

Moisture testing measures hydrostatic vapor pressure in concrete. Slabs with high moisture emissions require a specialized moisture-blocking epoxy primer to stop bubbling.

Next Question (What is an acceptable moisture level for standard epoxy?): Standard epoxy requires moisture vapor emission rates (MVER) below 3 lbs per 1,000 sq ft per 24 hours; higher rates require specialized moisture-mitigation primers.

The geographic positioning of Rochester within the Chehalis River basin presents distinct subterranean challenges for concrete floor coatings. Glacial outwash gravels and low-lying prairie topography create shallow water tables that fluctuate significantly between dry summers and rainy winter months. When rainwater saturates the sub-base, water moves upward through porous concrete via capillary action and hydrostatic pressure.

If an epoxy coating is installed over a concrete slab without proper moisture evaluation, rising vapor pressure will eventually become trapped directly beneath the impermeable coating film. Over weeks or months, hydrostatic pressure forces vapor bubbles against the resin, resulting in osmotic blistering, pinholes, and widespread coating delamination.

To eliminate moisture-related failures, professional contractors perform comprehensive diagnostic moisture testing prior to installation. We utilize in-situ Relative Humidity (RH) probes according to ASTM F2170 standards, which measure the internal humidity within the core of the slab. We also perform calcium chloride tests (ASTM F1869) to calculate the precise pounds of moisture vapor leaving 1,000 square feet of concrete over a 24-hour period.

When elevated moisture emissions are detected, we apply an ASTM F3010-compliant moisture-mitigation epoxy primer. These specialized high-density resin formulations bond tenaciously to damp concrete capillaries and tolerate vapor pressure up to 25 pounds or 100% in-situ relative humidity. By anchoring this barrier into the slab, the decorative flake and polyaspartic layers above remain permanently protected against hydrostatic bubbling.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if my garage slab has high moisture?

Tape a two-foot square piece of clear plastic tightly to your concrete for 48 hours. If droplets condense on the plastic or the concrete darkens, high moisture vapor is present.

Does a vapor barrier beneath the original concrete eliminate moisture risk?

While sub-slab vapor barriers help, older homes often lack them, and contractors frequently puncture them during utility installation, making direct testing essential.
